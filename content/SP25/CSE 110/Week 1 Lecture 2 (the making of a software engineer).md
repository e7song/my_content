#### Fact #1
- the most important factor in improving software development is not the tools and techniques used by programmers, but the quality of the programmers themselves
- it's always a people problem
	- junior developers struggle the most with soft skills
	- the major issues of software development are human and not technical
		- why should we care?

#### Even economic data supports it
- the data shows that being better programmers is the most important part

#### 2x, 10x, or 28x?
- not trusting AI implicitly
- strive for excellence and be self-aware of your current status
	- everything takes time and effort
- sometimes, it is more important to admit that you don't know something than to pretend that you do
	- the truth is that we all don't know something
	- the important part is knowing how to figure these things out
- software development is a team sport
	- it is not about individual performance; it is about team success
	- the lack of coherent teamwork, nullified the gains of individual effort or brilliance
		- the team gets better together

#### Great teams > great players
- Gresham's law for software development
- net negative producing programmer (NNPP)
	- overperformers that make their team miserable
	- people who cause more harm than good

#### A reasonable conclusion
- yes, sometimes people are better
- don't ruin your team with nonsense behavior
- careful with factualizing what is going on in tech without some skepticism

##### Fact #5
- software tools and technique improvements $\rightarrow$ 5-35% percent increase in productivity or quality
	- online grifters tend to have reasons for hyping things up

#### Fact #6
- learning a new tool, etc. lowers initial productivity and quality initially--the gains come later
	- RDD--resume driven development...
		- adds risk to the project
- need to be mindful about staying up to date but also stable
- don't want to have a million tools
	- get good at a tool; get new tools when the tools you are good at are not sufficient

#### Postel's law for yourself
- robustness principle
	- conservative in what you emit and liberal in what you accept
- roughly applied to our effort
	- you can control what you do
	- be mindful of what you accept

#### Attitude first
- you must want to be a better programmer
- it will take time and effort to become a better programmer
- like any thing, to do good X you have to be care about X
- motivation is the key driver to project success

#### Selfish programmers
- think of themselves more than others
- take more pride in their tools, materials, processes, and other aspects of the endeavor personal to them as opposed to their users
	- are we doing things solely for ourselves or in service of others?
#### The need for practice
- need to keep practicing
- generally, once you reach an acceptable performance and automaticity, the additional years of practice don't lead to improvement
- the truth is
	- 10 years of 1 year experience rather than 10 years of experience
